Current focus

My research focuses on self-improving agents: how they can generate useful experience, learn continuously from interaction, and become more capable over time. I’m particularly interested in the learning signals that enable this—rewards, verifiers, credit assignment, and trajectory-level evaluation—and in making them robust to reward hacking and other failure modes.

01

Agentic post-training for long-horizon agents

I work across the post-training loop—supervised fine-tuning, on-policy data, reinforcement learning, synthetic task generation, environments, and evaluation—to improve agents that plan, use tools, recover from mistakes, and make progress over many steps. I’m especially interested in trajectory-level credit assignment and whether learned behaviors generalize across tasks, environments, and tool interfaces.

02

Rewards, verifiers, and process signals

Automated rewards make large-scale RL possible, but they also create new failure modes. I build and stress-test verifiers, compare outcome and trajectory-level signals, and look for proxy optimization, reward hacking, and brittle evaluation before those signals are used to train a stronger policy.

Prime Intellect · Agentic RL

Stress-testing rewards for long-horizon agents

Built an experimental workflow for testing whether an automated reward remains trustworthy under optimization pressure. The approach uses controlled probes, defense ablations, hardened re-scoring, and trajectory inspection to surface verifier brittleness and reward hacking before scaling an RL run. I open-sourced the reference implementation for SWE-style agent tasks and presented the work at Ray Summit 2026.

Another focus of my work is scalable synthetic task generation for training and evaluating long-horizon agents. I build pipelines for generating realistic, multi-step workflows in simulated tool environments, with structured world states and programmatically verifiable outcomes. I’m particularly interested in generating tasks that span a wide range of difficulty and expose failures in planning, tool use, and long-horizon execution.
